Why now

When Jupiter enters the Lion, the waters are called holy.

Every twelve years, Brihaspati — the planet Jupiter — finishes its slow walk through the zodiac and steps into Simha, the sign of the Lion.

Where this turning falls upon the Godavari, the old texts say the water carries a trace of amrita, the nectar of deathlessness. So the people come — not because a calendar tells them to, but because the sky has moved.

Jupiter takes close to twelve years to circle the sun. That long rhythm sets the Simhastha. Miss it, and the next is a childhood away.

Two waters, one river

The bathing is shared between two sacred kunds.

Unlike other Kumbh sites, here the holy bath is held in two places — the Godavari at Nashik, and the river's own source at Trimbakeshwar, about thirty kilometres west.

Nashik

Ram Kund · on the Godavari

In the heart of the city, where the river gathers.

Distance
Nashik city centre, Panchavati
The water
Ram Kund, long tied to the memory of Lord Ram
Who gathers
The Vaishnava (Bairagi) akharas — followers of Vishnu and Ram — are traditionally recorded as bathing here

Trimbakeshwar

Kushavarta Kund · the river's source

Where the Godavari is born, beside a Jyotirlinga.

Distance
~30 km west of Nashik
The water
Kushavarta Kund, the symbolic source of the Godavari
Who gathers
The Shaiva akharas — and the Udasin and Sanyasi orders who follow Shiva — are traditionally recorded as bathing here, beside one of the twelve Jyotirlingas

A note on the orders. The pairing above — Shaiva at Trimbakeshwar, Vaishnava at Nashik — is the commonly recorded arrangement, traced to a separation made by the Peshwas after deadly clashes between the sects centuries ago. Even so, accounts differ in their detail and arrangements can change from one mela to the next. Treat this as background, and confirm the current plan with local authorities before you travel.

Before you go

Travel gently. Travel prepared.

Reaching Nashik

RailNashik Road station, about 8 km from the old city, on the Mumbai–Bhusawal line. Expect special trains and very heavy demand around the bathing days — book early.
RoadBy bus or car from Mumbai (~165 km) or Pune (~210 km) on national highways. Roads near the ghats close to traffic on peak days; park on the outskirts and walk in.
AirNearest airports are Nashik (Ozar), with limited flights, and Mumbai (~170 km) for wider connections, then onward by train or road.

Where crowds gather

The densest crowds form at Ram Kund in Nashik and Kushavarta Kund in Trimbakeshwar on the three royal bathing days. Approach roads begin to fill many hours before dawn.

If your aim is calm darshan rather than the spectacle, the long stretches between the peaks are far easier — and just as meaningful.

Staying safe in the crowd

  • Agree a meeting point and a time before you set out — phone networks falter when millions gather in one place.
  • Give anyone travelling with elders or children a written card with a contact number.
  • Move with the flow, never against it. Follow marshals, barriers, and the marked bathing slots.
  • Note the nearest first-aid and lost-persons points when you arrive.

What to carry

  • Water
  • Light food
  • A hat
  • Basic medicines
  • A small torch
  • Cash in small notes
  • Dry clothes in a waterproof bag
  • Footwear you can slip off
  • A power bank
  • ID

The gentler days

If you can, avoid the three royal-bath peaks. The mela runs for the better part of a year. On an ordinary morning the ghats are calm, the queues short, and the river quiet — no less holy for being unhurried. For many, this is the truer pilgrimage.

Millions will come — and each will stand alone with the Godavari.
